Best Haircuts for Men With Oval Faces

Oval faces are the most versatile face shape — almost every haircut works. Here's how to choose from all those options, and the few mistakes to avoid.

October 6, 20263 min readBlue Shadows Barber Studio

If your face is a little longer than it is wide, with a jaw slightly narrower than your cheekbones and soft, balanced edges, you probably have an oval face. Barbers often call it the most versatile face shape, because its natural proportions are already balanced. That means you can pull off almost any haircut — the question becomes which one suits your hair and your life.

How to know you have an oval face

  • Your face is longer than it is wide, but not dramatically so.
  • Your forehead is slightly wider than your chin.
  • Your jawline is rounded rather than square or sharply pointed.
  • Your cheekbones are the widest part of your face, but only slightly.

Still unsure? Our face shape guide walks through all six shapes.

Best haircuts for oval faces

Textured quiff

Volume on top, swept up and back, with a mid fade or taper on the sides. Shows off balanced proportions and works with most hair types.

Classic side part

Clean and timeless. An oval face doesn't need the part to do any correcting, so you can choose a soft natural part or a sharper, more defined one.

Textured crop

Short, choppy, and modern. Works well with a skin or mid fade and needs almost no styling.

Buzz cut

Oval faces are among the best suited to a buzz cut, because there's nothing to balance. Add a fade and a crisp lineup for definition.

Slick back

Pulling the hair back shows off the whole face. Pair it with a low fade for a modern look, or keep the sides longer for a classic feel.

Medium-length flow

Oval faces can carry longer hair well. Shoulder-length or tucked-behind-the-ear styles look natural, as long as the neckline and around the ears are kept tidy.

Curtains or middle part

Centre-parted styles are less forgiving on some face shapes, but an oval face handles them easily.

What to watch out for

With an oval face, the main risk isn't choosing the "wrong" cut — it's accidentally changing your proportions:

  • Very heavy fringes can make the face look rounder by hiding the forehead.
  • Extreme height on top can push an oval face toward looking long.
  • Very wide sides can make the face look rounder.

Keep things in proportion — the 3-2-1 rule is a good reference — and you'll look balanced in almost any style.

Beards for oval faces

Just like haircuts, most beard styles suit oval faces. Short boxed beards, heavy stubble, and full beards all work. Keep the cheek line natural and the neckline crisp, and avoid styles that add a lot of length at the chin, which can make the face look long.

Choosing among all the options

When everything suits your face, other factors decide: your hair type, your hairline, how much time you'll spend styling, and what your work and weekends look like. That's what a good consultation is for.
