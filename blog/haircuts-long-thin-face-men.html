Best Haircuts for Men With Long, Thin Faces

A long, narrow face looks best with cuts that add width and avoid extra height. Here are the styles that work — and the ones that don't.

October 6, 20263 min readBlue Shadows Barber Studio

A long or thin face — sometimes called an oblong or rectangular face shape — is longer than it is wide, with a forehead, cheekbones, and jaw of similar width. It's a strong, often angular face shape. The goal of a good haircut isn't to hide that, but to balance it: adding a bit of width and avoiding anything that makes the face look even longer.

How to tell if you have a long face

Pull your hair back and look straight into a mirror. If your face is noticeably longer than it is wide — roughly one and a half times or more — and your cheekbones and jaw are about the same width as your forehead, you likely have a long or oblong face. Not sure? Our face shape guide walks through it.

The principles

  • Avoid extra height on top. Tall quiffs and pompadours stretch the face further.
  • Keep some fullness on the sides. Very tight, high skin fades narrow the head and exaggerate length.
  • Use fringes and forward styling. Hair that falls forward onto the forehead visually shortens the face.
  • Go for horizontal lines. Side parts and side-swept styles draw the eye across, not up.

Best haircuts for long faces

The side part with a low taper

A classic for a reason. Medium length on top, swept to the side, with a low taper that keeps some weight on the sides. The horizontal sweep balances the length of the face beautifully.

Textured crop with a fringe

A short, choppy top pushed forward to create a fringe across the forehead. Pair it with a low or mid fade rather than a high skin fade. This is one of the most flattering modern cuts for a long face.

Ivy League / short side-swept

Shorter than a full side part, with the front swept to the side. Neat, versatile, and easy to style for work.

The Caesar cut

Short and even with a straight, forward-brushed fringe. It shortens the forehead and gives the face a more balanced look.

Medium-length, natural texture

If you have some wave or curl, letting it grow to medium length with volume at the sides adds width. Keep it shaped so it doesn't get heavy.

Styles to approach carefully

  • High pompadours and quiffs — add height where you don't need it.
  • High skin fades with a tall top — the combination of tight sides and height elongates the face.
  • Very long, straight hair with a centre part — creates vertical lines that emphasize length.

None of these are impossible — a skilled barber can adapt almost any style — but they need careful adjustments to work.

Beards for long faces

A beard can be a powerful tool for balancing a long face, as long as it's shaped the right way:

  • Keep it fuller on the sides and shorter on the chin, to add width without adding length.
  • Avoid long, pointed beards that extend the chin downward.
  • Short boxed beards and heavy stubble are usually the most flattering.
