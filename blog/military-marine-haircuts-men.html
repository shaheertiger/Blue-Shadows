Military and Marine-Style Haircuts for Men: High and Tight, Crew Cut, and More

Clean, sharp, and zero fuss. Military-inspired haircuts have been popular for decades for a reason. Here's a guide to the classics and how to wear them today.

October 6, 20263 min readBlue Shadows Barber Studio

Military haircuts were designed to be practical: clean, uniform, easy to maintain, and suitable under a helmet. Those same qualities make them popular far beyond the armed forces. Athletes, busy professionals, outdoor types, and anyone who wants to look sharp with minimal effort keep coming back to them.

Here's a guide to the classic military-inspired cuts, including the famous Marine-style high and tight.

The high and tight

The cut most associated with Marines. The sides and back are clipped very short — often down to the skin — high up the head, leaving a short strip of hair on top. The transition is sharp and dramatic.

  • Look: bold, athletic, disciplined.
  • Maintenance: very easy day to day, but needs a cleanup every one to two weeks to stay sharp.
  • Ask for: a high skin fade or high and tight, with about half an inch to an inch on top.

The high and tight recon

An even more extreme version where the top is also very short and the shaved sides go even higher, leaving a small patch on top.

The crew cut

A softer military classic. Short, tapered sides with slightly longer hair on top — often a little longer at the front so it can be pushed up or to the side.

  • Look: clean, approachable, classic.
  • Maintenance: easy; a cut every three to four weeks.
  • Ask for: a crew cut with a taper on the sides, about an inch on top, slightly longer at the front.

The buzz cut

The most basic military cut: one clipper length all over. Variations include the induction cut (very short all over) and the butch cut (slightly longer, even length).

  • Look: minimal and confident.
  • Maintenance: zero styling; a cleanup every two to three weeks for crisp edges.
  • Ask for: a buzz cut with a specific guard (for example, a #2 all over), plus a sharp lineup and neckline. Add a fade on the sides for a modern version.

The Ivy League

A longer crew cut with enough length on top to create a side part. Originally popular with university students in the 1950s, it's the most office-friendly of the military family.

  • Look: polished and professional.
  • Maintenance: a cut every three to four weeks; a little product to style.
  • Ask for: an Ivy League with a tapered side, about one to two inches on top, and a side part.

The flat top

A classic military style where the hair on top is cut to stand up and form a flat, level surface. It takes real skill to cut and works best with thick, straight or coarse hair.

Who suits military haircuts?

  • Strong head shapes look great with shorter military cuts like the high and tight or buzz.
  • Square and oval faces suit almost every military style.
  • Round faces benefit from the height of a high and tight or crew cut.
  • Thinning hair often looks best very short — a buzz or crew cut is a confident choice.

Our face shape guide can help you decide.

Making it modern

Today's versions of military cuts often add a skin fade, a sharp lineup, a textured top, or a neat beard to soften the look. The core idea stays the same: clean, structured, and low maintenance.
