How Often Should You Get a Haircut? A Milton Barber's Schedule Guide

The right haircut schedule depends on your style, your hair, and how sharp you want to look. Here's a simple breakdown, cut by cut.

October 6, 20263 min readBlue Shadows Barber Studio

It's one of the most common questions we hear in the chair: "How often should I be coming in?" The honest answer is that it depends — but not in a vague way. Hair grows about half an inch (roughly 1.25 cm) a month on average. On a cut where the sides are only a few millimetres long, that growth shows up fast. On a longer style, you've got more time.

Here's a practical guide based on what you're wearing.

Skin fades and high fades: every 2–3 weeks

Skin fades look incredible for about two weeks. After that, the blend starts to soften, the lineup loses its edge, and the contrast that makes the cut look sharp fades away. If you love the fresh-out-of-the-chair look, every two weeks is ideal. Most clients find three weeks is a good balance.

Low fades and tapers: every 3–4 weeks

Lower fades and tapers grow out more gracefully because there's less contrast between the short and long sections. You can comfortably stretch to three or four weeks and still look tidy. That makes them a favourite with busy Milton commuters — see our commuter cut guide.

Classic scissor cuts: every 4–6 weeks

Longer, scissor-cut styles — side parts, pompadours, textured mid-length cuts — can go four to six weeks between visits. You'll want a trim around the ears and neckline in that window to keep the shape from getting heavy.

Growing your hair out: every 6–8 weeks

Counterintuitive but true: growing hair still needs regular trims. Every six to eight weeks, a barber can clean up the neckline and around the ears, take out bulk, and shape the growth so you skip the awkward stage. Skipping cuts entirely usually means months of looking messy instead of intentionally growing.

Buzz cuts: every 2–4 weeks

A buzz is simple, but the edges are what make it look sharp. A quick lineup and neckline cleanup every two to four weeks keeps it looking deliberate rather than overgrown.

Beards: every 2–4 weeks

Short, defined beards need a line-up every two to three weeks. Longer beards can stretch to four weeks, with daily oil and brushing in between. Pairing your beard trim with your haircut is the easiest way to keep both on schedule.

Kids and students: every 3–5 weeks

Kids' hair often grows faster than you'd expect, and active kids and teens with short fades will need a cleanup every three to four weeks. Longer kids' cuts can stretch to about five. Plan around school photos, sports pictures, and holidays.

Signs you're overdue

  • Your hair is touching the top of your ears or your collar when it normally doesn't.
  • The neckline has grown fuzzy, or the beard line has crept down your neck.
  • Your usual styling routine suddenly takes twice as long.
  • Your hair feels heavy and won't sit the way it did.
  • You've started wearing a hat "just because."

The easiest trick: rebook before you leave

The people who always look sharp aren't more disciplined — they just book their next appointment before they walk out the door. Choose a day and time that fits your schedule, put it in your calendar, and never think about it again.
