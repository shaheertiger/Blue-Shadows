The Best Hair Products for Men: What You Actually Need in Your Routine

Pomade, paste, clay, cream — the product shelf is confusing. Here's what each one does, which hair types it suits, and how to use it without overdoing it.

October 6, 20263 min readBlue Shadows Barber Studio

A great haircut gets you most of the way. The right product gets you the rest. But walk down any grooming aisle and you'll find pomades, pastes, clays, waxes, creams, gels, and sprays that all seem to promise the same thing. Here's a simple guide to what each one actually does.

The two things that matter: hold and shine

Almost every styling product can be described by two qualities:

  • Hold — how firmly it keeps your hair in place, from light and flexible to strong and stiff.
  • Shine — how much it reflects light, from completely matte to high gloss.

Pick the hold your style needs and the finish you like, and you've narrowed the shelf down to a couple of options.

Styling products, explained

Clay

Hold: medium to strong. Shine: matte.
Clay adds texture and thickness, making it ideal for textured crops, messy styles, and fine hair that needs body. It's the most popular product for modern short cuts.

Paste

Hold: medium. Shine: low to medium.
The all-rounder. Paste is flexible, easy to restyle during the day, and works for most short to medium styles.

Pomade

Hold: medium to strong. Shine: medium to high.
The classic choice for slick backs, side parts, and pompadours. Water-based pomades wash out easily; oil-based pomades last longer but are harder to remove.

Wax

Hold: strong. Shine: low to medium.
Great for defined, piece-y styles and shorter hair that needs to stay put. A little goes a long way.

Cream

Hold: light. Shine: natural.
Perfect for longer hair, curls, and anyone who wants a soft, natural look with a bit of control and frizz reduction.

Sea salt spray

Hold: light. Shine: matte.
Adds volume and beachy texture. Spray into damp hair before blow-drying, or use as a pre-styler under clay or paste.

Gel

Hold: strong. Shine: high (wet look).
Less common today, but still useful for very defined styles and for some curl routines. Modern gels are much less crunchy than the ones from the early 2000s.

Matching product to hair type

  • Fine or thin hair: matte clay or sea salt spray to build volume. Avoid heavy pomades that weigh hair down.
  • Thick hair: pomade or strong paste to control bulk.
  • Straight hair: clay or paste for texture; straight hair can look flat without it. See our straight hair guide.
  • Curly or coily hair: creams, leave-in conditioners, and curl products that define without crunch. Read our curly hair guide.

How to apply product properly

  1. Start with clean, towel-dried hair (damp for creams and sprays; nearly dry for clays and pastes).
  2. Use less than you think — about the size of a pea or a fingertip.
  3. Warm it between your palms until it's almost invisible.
  4. Apply from the back to the front, so the front doesn't get overloaded.
  5. Work it to the roots, then shape with your fingers or a comb.
  6. Add a tiny bit more only if you need it.

Don't skip the basics

Styling products get the attention, but your foundation matters just as much:

  • A good shampoo — gentle enough to use a few times a week without stripping your scalp.
  • A conditioner — yes, even for short hair. It keeps hair and scalp healthy.
  • Beard oil, if you have a beard — especially through Ontario winters.
