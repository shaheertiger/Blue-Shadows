Your Child's First Barbershop Haircut: A Milton Parent's Guide

Clippers are loud, the chair is tall, and there's a stranger with scissors. Here's how Milton parents can turn a first haircut into a good memory.

October 6, 20263 min readBlue Shadows Barber Studio

Milton is a young town. Walk through any neighbourhood on a Saturday and you'll see strollers, scooters, and plenty of little heads that are ready for their first real haircut. For a lot of families, that first barbershop visit is a milestone — and for a lot of kids, it's a little scary.

We've seen every version of a first haircut, from kids who sit perfectly still to kids who need three breaks and a snack. Here's what actually helps.

Prepare them before the day

  • Talk about it in advance. Explain what will happen: a cape, a spray bottle, a comb, scissors, and clippers that buzz. Surprises are what make kids nervous.
  • Play barbershop at home. Let them "cut" a stuffed animal's hair with a comb, or let them run a (switched-off) electric toothbrush near their ear so the buzzing feels familiar.
  • Bring them along to someone else's cut. If a parent or older sibling has an appointment, let the little one watch. Seeing it's no big deal goes a long way.

Pick the right time

Timing makes a bigger difference than anything else.

  • Book after a nap and a meal. A tired, hungry kid in a barber chair is a tough combination.
  • Mornings are usually best for toddlers. For school-age kids, weekends or right after school can work well.
  • Avoid rushing. Don't squeeze a first haircut in before a birthday party or a family photo session. Give it room.

What to bring

  • A favourite small toy or a tablet with a show they love — distraction is your friend.
  • A snack for afterward (not during — hair and crackers don't mix).
  • A photo of the style you're hoping for, or simply a sense of how short you want it.
  • A spare shirt. Tiny hairs get everywhere, and itchy kids are unhappy kids.

In the chair

A good barber will move at your child's pace. A few things that help:

  • Sitting on a parent's lap is completely fine for the very first cut. Comfort beats perfect posture.
  • Scissors first, clippers later. For nervous kids, starting with scissors and a comb lets them get used to the chair before the buzzing starts.
  • Short breaks are OK. A quick pause to look in the mirror or wave at a parent can reset the mood.
  • Keep your own energy calm. Kids read their parents. If you're relaxed and excited, they usually follow.

Simple, kid-friendly styles

For a first cut, simple is smart. A neat trim that cleans up the ears and neckline while keeping some length on top is a gentle start. As kids get older and more confident, they often want something with more personality — a textured crop, a low fade, or a small design for the first day of school. Our back-to-school guide has more ideas for school-age kids.

Save the moment

Take a quick before-and-after photo. Many parents also save a small lock of hair from the very first cut. Just let us know at the start of the appointment and we'll set some aside for you.
