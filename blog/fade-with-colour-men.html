Fades With Colour: How to Add Colour to Your Men's Haircut

A fade is a great canvas for colour — short hair, quick refreshes, and plenty of room to experiment. Here's how to choose the right look and keep it fresh.

October 6, 20263 min readBlue Shadows Barber Studio

Adding colour to a fade is one of the easiest ways to refresh your look without changing your haircut. And because the sides are cut short every few weeks, a fade is a low-commitment canvas: if you don't love a colour, it's not around for long. Here's how to think about it.

Why fades and colour work so well together

  • The contrast does the work. A fade naturally creates contrast between short sides and a longer top — colour on top makes that contrast stand out even more.
  • Short hair colours quickly. Less length means less product, shorter appointments, and a lower price.
  • Regrowth is less obvious. Colour on short hair gets trimmed away with your regular cuts.

Colour ideas for fades

Natural darkening

A subtle tint that deepens your natural colour. It makes the top look fuller, sharpens the contrast with the fade, and covers early grey. The most popular starting point for men new to colour.

Grey blending

Softens grey on top and at the temples while keeping things natural. Great for men in their 30s and up who want to look sharp without looking "coloured." Read more in our grey blending guide.

Warm brown or copper tones

A slightly warmer shade adds dimension to dark hair, especially on textured crops and curls where it catches the light.

Blonde or platinum top

A bold, high-contrast look: platinum or blonde on top with a dark or skin fade on the sides. Requires lightening, so it's a bigger commitment — especially for dark hair.

Coloured design or part line

For a subtle statement, add colour to a design or part line cut into the fade. Popular for events, holidays, and showing team colours.

Beard colour to match

If your hair is coloured, consider colouring your beard to match, so the two blend together naturally. Beards often grey faster than hair, so matching them can make a big difference.

Before you colour

  • Be realistic with dark hair. Going from very dark to very light takes lightening, multiple steps, and more aftercare.
  • Think about your skin tone. Cool tones and warm tones suit different complexions. A good consultation will steer you right.
  • Mention any sensitivities to products or dyes before you start.
  • Consider your workplace. Natural tints work everywhere; bold colours might not.

Keeping colour fresh

  • Use a gentle, sulphate-free shampoo to slow fading.
  • Wash in lukewarm water — hot water opens the hair cuticle and lets colour escape.
  • Protect from sun and chlorine — a cap outdoors and a rinse after swimming help.
  • Pair touch-ups with your cuts. Short colour usually looks its best for three to five weeks — about one or two haircut cycles.
