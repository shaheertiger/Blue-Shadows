Low, Mid, High, Skin, Taper: The Fade Guide Every Milton Guy Should Read

"Fade on the sides, a bit off the top" leaves a lot of room for interpretation. Learn the language and you'll walk out with exactly the cut you pictured.

October 6, 20263 min readBlue Shadows Barber Studio

The fade is the most requested haircut at barbershops across Milton — and also the most misunderstood. "Just a fade" can mean a dozen different cuts depending on who's holding the clippers. Knowing a few terms helps you explain what you want and get it consistently.

What a fade actually is

A fade is a haircut where the hair on the sides and back gradually gets shorter as it moves down the head, blending smoothly from longer hair on top to very short hair (or skin) near the ears and neckline. The two things that define any fade are:

  • Where it starts — how high up the head the shortest point begins to blend.
  • How short it gets — down to the skin, or to a short clipper length.

Fade heights

Low fade

The blend starts just above the ears and follows the hairline around the back. It's subtle, clean, and professional — a great choice if you want a fresh look without high contrast. Low fades also grow out gracefully, which makes them popular with busy commuters.

Mid fade

The blend starts around the middle of the sides, roughly at the temple. It's the most versatile option: noticeable enough to look modern, balanced enough for almost any face shape and hair type.

High fade

The blend starts high, near the parietal ridge (where the head starts to curve toward the top). It creates strong contrast and a bold, sharp look. High fades suit people who want a clean, athletic style and work especially well with short tops or textured crops.

Fade styles

Skin fade (bald fade)

Any fade that goes all the way down to the skin. The sharpest, cleanest-looking option, but it shows growth fastest — expect to need a cleanup every two to three weeks to keep it crisp.

Drop fade

The fade line dips down behind the ear and curves toward the nape, following the shape of the head. It adds shape and movement and pairs well with curly or textured tops.

Burst fade

The fade radiates in a semicircle around the ear, leaving length at the back of the neck. It's a popular choice for curly hair, mohawks, and longer styles where you want definition around the ear without cutting the back short.

Taper

A taper is a more conservative cousin of the fade. Instead of blending the whole side, it shortens the hair mainly at the sideburns and neckline, leaving more length elsewhere. It's classic, office-friendly, and grows out beautifully.

How to choose

  • Lifestyle: If you want low maintenance, choose a low fade or taper. If you're happy to visit every two weeks, a skin fade looks incredible.
  • Head and face shape: Higher fades add height and length to the face; lower fades keep things balanced and soft. Our face shape guide covers this in detail.
  • Hair type: Curly and coily hair pairs naturally with burst and drop fades. Fine, straight hair often looks fuller with a lower fade that keeps some weight on the sides.
  • Workplace: Most offices are fine with any clean fade, but a taper or low fade is the safest choice if you're unsure.

What to say in the chair

Here's a simple script that gets you 90% of the way:

"I'd like a [low / mid / high] [skin / #1 / #2] fade, with about [length] left on top, [natural / sharp] lineup, and [blended / squared] neckline."

And always bring a photo. Two people's idea of a "mid fade" can be very different — a picture removes the guesswork.
